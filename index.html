The Pyrologist's Field Dictionary & Carbon Handbook

Pyrolysis

Thermochemical decomposition of organic matter at elevated temperatures in the absolute absence of oxygen (anoxic atmosphere). It splits biomass into solids (biochar), liquids (bio-oil), and fuel gases (syngas).

Permanence (H:C Ratio)

The long-term chemical durability of carbon in soil. Biochars with an atomic Hydrogen-to-Organic Carbon ($H:C_{org}$) ratio of less than 0.4 offer superb structural permanence, persisting in the ground for several hundred to thousands of years.

Cation Exchange Capacity (CEC)

A measure of how well soil can retain and exchange crucial mineral nutrients (calcium, magnesium, potassium). Biochar boosts CEC dramatically by introducing negative electrostatic charge centers onto its porous carbon matrix.

CO₂ Removal Certificates (CORCs)

High-quality environmental assets traded on voluntary markets. One CORC represents one physical tonne of certified, net-negative CO₂ successfully sequestered away from the atmospheric cycle.

Syngas Loop & Volatile Recovery

The process of routing hot condensable and non-condensable fuel gases (mainly CO, $H_2$, $CH_4$) back to heat the reactor. This thermal synergy greatly lowers required outside energy inputs, decreasing life-cycle emissions.

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)

A framework to count net-sequestration. It deducts emissions from biomass harvesting, transportation logistics, grinding, kiln preheating, and packaging from the total raw carbon sequestered.
